## Draft A — direct, founder voice

> Migraine tracking often leaves symptoms, sleep, and weather scattered across different places—with no easy way to look back and see what kept lining up.
>
> I built Gaia Eyes to put that context together. On iPhone, you can log a migraine or another symptom, add possible exposures, optionally connect Apple Health, and compare those entries with sleep, HRV, pressure, humidity, air quality, and other environmental signals over time.
>
> It does not tell you what caused a migraine. It gives you a clearer personal record of what was happening around it, so recurring patterns are easier to review.
>
> The core app is free and ad-free, and Apple Health is optional: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gaia-eyes/id6761451455
>
> If you track migraines, what is the one thing you wish a tracker made easier when you are actually symptomatic? No need to share private health details.

Use Draft A only if the group permits founder/product links and feedback questions.

## Draft B — discussion first, link optional

> For anyone who tracks migraines: do you ever remember the pain but not what sleep, pressure, humidity, or the rest of the day looked like around it?
>
> That is the problem I am trying to make less tedious with Gaia Eyes. It keeps symptom and exposure entries together with optional Apple Health and local/environmental context, so you can review your own patterns over time instead of trying to remember everything later.
>
> It is currently available for iPhone. The core experience is free and ad-free, and a wearable is optional.
>
> Which part of migraine logging is hardest when you are in the middle of one: starting the entry, answering too many questions, or remembering details later?

## Reusable first-value explanation

Use this in an approved reply when someone asks what they can do first:

> Start with one thing you already notice—such as a migraine, headache, fatigue, or poor sleep. Log it when it happens. Gaia Eyes can keep that entry beside optional Apple Health data and current local/environmental context. As you add entries over time, you can review what repeatedly appears together. You do not need a wearable or Plus to begin, and Gaia Eyes is not deciding the cause for you.

Suggested first session:

1. Install the released iPhone app and complete the relevant onboarding choices.
2. Connect Apple Health only if desired; the app remains usable without it.
3. Review Today's Read and the four primary gauges without treating a possible symptom as a diagnosis.
4. Log one real symptom, exposure, or Daily Check-In.
5. Return to Patterns after more entries accumulate; do not promise an immediate conclusion from one entry.
