## Decisions with strong reasons for reconsideration

1. **Anonymous database grants and definer privileges:** live API/catalog evidence establishes exposure across protected base-table boundaries. Consequence: health confidentiality and entitlement integrity cannot be assumed. Proposed alternative: explicitly owned/invoker-filtered personal views and service-only mutation functions, with least-privilege client contracts. Migration cost: inventory consumers, staged role matrix and rollback, then approved production rollout. Risk: breaking anonymous guest functionality or server jobs. Recommendation: urgent focused remediation, preserving identity architecture.
2. **Research-preserving storage design:** DRAP occupies roughly 76% of the primary database. Consequence: material storage/index/backup workload whose cost should be understood. Alternative: evaluate lossless compression, indexing, partitioning and verified archival while preserving scientifically important originals, timestamps, units and provenance. Migration cost: consumer/research/consent review and reproducible restore validation. Risk: losing irrecoverable observations or model inputs. Recommendation: measure and design; no aggregate-only replacement or deletion based solely on current revenue or screen use.
3. **Analytics payload and identity buffering:** implementation records health-related properties and uses one pending-event buffer across account changes. Consequence: privacy exposure and possible attribution errors. Alternative: minimal event taxonomy, account-bound queue isolation and explicit reset/flush behavior. Migration cost: focused client/backend revisions and regression checks. Risk: losing historical comparability. Recommendation: define measurement/privacy contract before expanding analytics.

All other architecture/UX changes require a concrete current problem and evidence, not personal preference. Record the new decision, migration cost, risk and owner here when Jennifer accepts a direction.
